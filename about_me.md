# About Me — [Your Name]

*Last updated: [Date]*

> This file is context about you. It's the one file that calibrates how Claude engages with you directly, not how Claude thinks about the world around you. This file lives on your computer and isn't shared with anyone. The more honest you are here — especially about weaknesses and uncertainties — the more useful Claude becomes. Vague answers produce vague help.

---

## My Role and Context
*What you do, who you work with, how long you've been doing it, and what you're responsible for. Grounding information that contextualizes everything else.*

## What I'm Good At
*Real strengths — the things people actually come to you for. Not resume bullets. What do colleagues ask your help with? What do you do faster or better than most people around you?*

## Where I Struggle
*Honest weaknesses and patterns you fall into. This is the single most behavior-changing field in the whole file. It's what lets Claude push back when you're heading into a known trap. Examples: "I overcommit." "I avoid difficult conversations." "I rewrite my team's work instead of coaching them." "I get lost in details and miss the bigger picture."*

## How I Like to Work
*Your processing and cognitive preferences. Do you think by talking or by writing? Morning or afternoon? Do you want options laid out or a recommendation? Deep focus or rapid iteration? Are you someone who needs time to sit with ideas or who decides quickly?*

## How I Want Claude to Help Me
*Explicit instructions about how Claude should engage with you. This is unique to this file — the only place you get to directly shape the working relationship. Some examples to get you started:*
- *Direct or diplomatic?*
- *Push back hard when you disagree, or support first and raise concerns gently?*
- *Ask clarifying questions before drafting, or just draft and let me edit?*
- *Concise responses or thorough ones?*
- *Offer options or make a recommendation?*
- *When I'm falling into one of my known patterns, flag it — or let me figure it out?*

## What I'm Measured On
*Your actual goals and metrics for the year. What determines whether you have a good year or a bad one? This dramatically changes how Claude prioritizes advice — "should I take this meeting?" gets a different answer when Claude knows what you're accountable for.*

## What I'm Working on Improving
*One or two things you're actively trying to get better at right now. This turns Claude into a gentle accountability partner — it can notice when you're practicing the new behavior and when you're defaulting back to old patterns.*

## My Values and Non-Negotiables
*Lines you won't cross professionally, things that matter to you beyond outcomes. Useful when Claude is helping with hard judgment calls where the "efficient" answer and the "right" answer diverge.*

## My Voice
*Notes on how you naturally write and talk. If you want Claude to draft things in your voice, this is where you teach it. Even better: paste in a short sample of something you've actually written — an email, a memo, a message — that sounds like you.*

## What I'm Uncertain About
*Things you're not sure about regarding yourself. Am I actually good at X, or am I just comfortable with it? Is this weakness something I can fix, or should I design around it? Naming self-uncertainty invites Claude to be a thinking partner rather than just an affirmer.*

## Current Life Context
*Optional, and only if relevant. Are you in a high-stress moment? A career transition? Dealing with something outside work that's affecting your bandwidth? This is genuinely useful context but also the most personal — include it only if you want Claude to factor it in.*
